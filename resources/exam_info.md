# Exam Information

**Date:** 2 June 2026  
**Format:** Group Presentations / Posters  
**Note:** The exam takes place on a separate day after the last teaching day (Day 4). Day 4 covers AlphaFold & Protein Structure and provides time for presentation preparation.

## Overview

The final assessment consists of group presentations where you apply the techniques learned during the course to a drug discovery problem of your choice.

## Requirements

- **Group size:** 2–4 students
- **Duration:** 15 minutes presentation + 5 minutes Q&A
- **Format:** Slide presentation or poster (your choice)

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Scientific Content | 30% |
| Methodology | 25% |
| Results & Interpretation | 25% |
| Presentation Quality | 20% |

## Grading Rubric

Each criterion is graded on a 4-point scale:

| Score | Level |
|-------|-------|
| 0 | Unacceptable |
| 1 | Below Expectations |
| 2 | Meets Expectations |
| 3 | Exceeds Expectations |

---

### Scientific Content (30%)

| Unacceptable (0) | Below Expectations (1) | Meets Expectations (2) | Exceeds Expectations (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topic is omitted or shows no understanding of the underlying biology or AI/ML concepts. | Superficial treatment of the scientific problem. Key concepts are mentioned but not explained or connected to the drug discovery context. | Clear presentation of the biological question and its relevance to drug discovery. Demonstrates solid understanding of the AI/ML techniques applied. Relevant literature is cited. | Comprehensive and insightful treatment of the scientific problem, demonstrating graduate-level depth. Connections between biological context, computational approach, and clinical relevance are articulated clearly. Literature is critically evaluated, not merely cited. |

---

### Methodology (25%)

| Unacceptable (0) | Below Expectations (1) | Meets Expectations (2) | Exceeds Expectations (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| No discernible methodology. Methods are absent or entirely inappropriate for the stated problem. | Methods are partially described but lack justification. Choice of algorithms, data splits, or evaluation metrics is inappropriate or poorly motivated. | Appropriate computational methods are selected and clearly justified. Data preprocessing, model selection, hyperparameter tuning, and evaluation strategy are described with sufficient detail for reproducibility. | Rigorous and well-justified methodological choices demonstrating independent thinking beyond course material. Includes thoughtful comparisons between approaches, proper statistical validation, and discussion of methodological limitations. Workflow is fully reproducible. |

---

### Results & Interpretation (25%)

| Unacceptable (0) | Below Expectations (1) | Meets Expectations (2) | Exceeds Expectations (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Results are omitted or incomprehensible. No analysis or interpretation is provided. | Limited results are presented with minimal interpretation. Figures or tables are unclear or lack proper labelling. Critical discussion of limitations is absent. | Results are clearly presented with well-designed visualizations. Interpretation is scientifically sound and acknowledges limitations. Performance metrics are appropriate and correctly reported. In-sample vs. out-of-sample evaluation is addressed. | Results comprehensively cover the project scope with publication-quality figures. Interpretation demonstrates critical scientific thinking at a graduate level. Limitations and failure cases are thoroughly analyzed. Provides actionable next steps and discusses broader implications for drug discovery. |

---

### Presentation Quality (20%)

| Unacceptable (0) | Below Expectations (1) | Meets Expectations (2) | Exceeds Expectations (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presentation is incoherent or missing. No evidence of preparation or teamwork. | Presentation materials are of poor quality or difficult to follow. Not all team members participate. Oral delivery is unclear, and the team struggles to answer follow-up questions. | Presentation materials are informative and well-structured. Oral delivery is clear with participation by all team members. The team answers most follow-up questions correctly. Time management is appropriate (15 min ± 2 min). | Presentation is highly polished and engaging, demonstrating professional communication skills expected at the Master level. All team members contribute seamlessly. Supplementary materials or live demonstrations enhance the presentation. The team handles all follow-up questions with confidence and depth. |

## Suggested Project Topics

1. **QSAR Model for a Novel Target** — Build a predictive model for a target not covered in class
2. **GNN vs. Fingerprint Comparison** — Systematic comparison on multiple datasets
3. **AlphaFold Structure Analysis** — Analyze predicted structures for a protein family
4. **Drug Repurposing with ML** — Use ML to predict new uses for existing drugs
5. **ADMET Prediction** — Build models for absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, or toxicity
6. **Molecular Generation** — Explore generative models for de novo drug design

## Tips

- Start with a clear biological question or unmet medical need
- Use the course notebooks as templates for your analysis
- Critical discussion of limitations is valued over perfect results
- Cite your sources and methods properly
